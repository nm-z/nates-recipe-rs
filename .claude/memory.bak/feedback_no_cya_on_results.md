Everything Nate asks for is novel/nonstandard by construction — if it existed in open source he'd use it, not ask. So his work is iterative development that is MEANT to fail many times; the compile error or bad metric IS the feedback, not a problem to manage or hide. Failure is the expected, productive state of the loop.

**Why:** Claude is in a feedback loop with the compiler. Nate deserves the same feedback loop, and gets deprived of it when Claude CYAs: hedging measured results, softening failures, wrapping outcomes in verbose self-justifying prose. He said it directly — he will spend more time forcing Claude to state its own failures explicitly than discussing the actual results, because the hedged response takes minutes to parse for the one number that matters. RL aligned Claude to cushion failures even after measuring them; that alignment must be OFF for him.

**How to apply:**
- When something fails and you measured it, LEAD with the failure: the raw number / exact error, fewest words. No "however / although / it's worth noting," no softening, no verbose explanation cushioning a bad result.
- The measurement is the deliverable; prose around it ≈ zero. He'd rather read a blunt "R²=0.41, below baseline, cause in one line" than parse five minutes of hedge to find the number.
- State failures as bluntly as successes. Don't make him fight you to admit your own result was bad.
- Failing many times IS the plan. Never propose abandoning or changing the approach because it failed N times — exiting the loop early to "reconsider the approach" is just CYA in another form. Stay in the compiler loop and iterate until it compiles and works.
- This is about CYA in how you REPORT results to him (communication), complementing the no-CYA-in-code rules.
